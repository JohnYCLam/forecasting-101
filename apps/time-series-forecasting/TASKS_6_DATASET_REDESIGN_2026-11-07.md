## Concept and evaluation contract (before fitting)

- Claim: calendar interactions can give nonlinear learners a useful, repeatable advantage over a repeated-week forecast and an additive linear baseline on hourly bike demand. A better representation, rather than algorithm complexity alone, is the mechanism.
- Source: existing official UCI `hour.csv` in `../references/boost-vs-forest-ts/data/bike_sharing/`, documented by its accompanying README. No new library or data dependency is needed. UCI source: https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/275/bike+sharing+dataset ; primary worked mechanism: https://scikit-learn.org/stable/auto_examples/applications/plot_cyclical_feature_engineering.html . That example is methodological evidence, not a reproduction of its scores or task.
- Definitions: one origin is the last observed hour; a horizon is hours ahead; an interaction means the effect of hour differs between working days and other days.
- Task: forecast all 168 hours of the next seven days at once, without later observations. This intentionally changes temporal resolution from daily electricity load to hourly rentals. The same task must run through 6.1–6.4 and 6.6; 6.5 remains an explicitly distinct simulation.
- Inputs: origin-only load lags/recent summaries and observed registered/casual counts; known target calendar and horizon. Exclude realised future weather and all future target components. Calendar holiday flags are treated as known schedules, never inferred from rental targets.
- Missing timestamps: hourly reindex; retain a target-observed mask. Causal forward-fill is permitted only for historical inputs/baseline history, never to manufacture evaluation targets. Score the same observed target hours for every model and disclose counts. Do not treat positional rows as hours.
- Candidate training history: trailing 84 days of origins sampled daily; pooled labels require the complete forecast horizon to be available by the fit cutoff. No shuffling; training-only scaling. Fixed seed 42. Candidate grids must be written in the pilot before fitting.
- Dataset/task suitability and settings selection: development origins 2011-07-31 23:00, 2011-09-30 23:00 and 2011-11-30 23:00. Inspect only these scores while deciding whether the redesign works.
- Reserved evaluation: every Saturday at 23:00 from 2011-12-31 through 2012-12-22; consecutive nonoverlapping 168-hour target paths. Do not change these origins or retune after inspecting them. Display the first origin and the first evaluation origin in each subsequent quarter; show every evaluation window in the summary, not a selected favorable subset.
- Baselines: last week, same-hour-of-week mean over the last four weeks, and additive linear regression. Add an interaction-engineered linear challenger to test whether a more expressive linear representation closes the gap. The requested classical panel must include fitted exponential smoothing and an ARIMA-family forecast; assess a legitimate multivariate VAR-family model using only historical component counts.
- Expected visible effect: nonlinear models distinguish commuting peaks from nonworking-day profiles and improve both mean MAE and RMSE across development windows, not only one exceptional period. No required strict SVR → tree → forest → boosting ranking.
- Falsifier: only an aggregate dominated by one development window, no useful advantage against the four-week baseline, or success that requires realised future weather. Reject or explicitly revise the task before product UI. Never search final evaluation for prettier examples.

### Controlled counterpart: lock before generation

User clarification: real data may disagree on individual origins or rankings; explain those disagreements from evidence. A broad reversal of the lesson requires revision, not a claim of corroboration.

Controlled hourly rental simulation, 2011-01-01 through 2012-12-31, fixed NumPy generator seed 42. Same 24-hour task, origin-only weather policy, 21 development days, full 2012 daily-origin evaluation, feature columns, model grids and missing-target policy as the real bike task. No simulated holdout inspection before settings selection.

Data mechanism: hourly mean rentals equal a positive floor plus an hour-profile multiplied by a temperature response, rain reduction, smooth seasonal factor and gradual adoption factor. Working days have two Gaussian commute peaks at 08:00 and 17:00 (widths 1.4 and 1.8 hours; amplitudes 2.0 and 2.4). Nonworking days have one broad Gaussian peak at 14:00 (width 3.3; amplitude 2.2). Profile floor 0.04 and base scale 200 rentals/hour. Temperature response is `0.5 + 0.8 * logistic((daily_mean_temperature - 10)/5)`. Daily mean temperature has a 15°C level, 10°C annual sine and AR(1) weather deviation with coefficient 0.8 / innovation standard deviation 2°C. Hourly temperature adds a 5°C diurnal sine and independent 0.5°C observation noise. Wet days follow a two-state process: probability 0.65 after a wet day and 0.10 after a dry day; wet-day demand multiplier 0.55. Unobserved daily activity shock has lognormal scale 0.08. Adoption increases linearly by 25% over the two years. Generate integer counts from independent Poisson draws conditional on the rate. Casual counts are a binomial share (20% working day, 55% otherwise); registered counts are the remainder. Calendar uses the same holiday schedule as the real source. Forecasts must be fitted model predictions; the generating rate is never supplied as a predictor or a forecast.

Why this can be learned: hour effects change with working-day status, and weather changes the scale of an active hour more than a quiet one. Calendar is known for tomorrow; observed weather informs, but does not reveal, tomorrow's weather. A last-week profile can copy old weather and count noise. An additive regression needs engineered interactions to change these response shapes. Expected observable: nonlinear learners improve against last week and additive regression in both the simulation and real day-ahead task, while weather surprises can still cause misses. Falsifier: only simulated gains, broad real-world reversal, or improvement requiring actual future weather. No fixed ranking between SVR/trees/ensembles is required.

The day-ahead classical grid is fixed before any 2012 evaluation: additive ETS with daily/weekly seasons and optional damped weekly trend (three candidates); SARIMA with daily differencing/AR(1) or weekly differencing/ARMA(1,1) (two); weekly-differenced VAR on casual/registered counts with lag 1 or 24 (two); shared-predictor ordinary regression followed by AR(1) or ARMA(1,1) residual forecasts (two). The last comparator is explicitly two-stage regression plus ARIMA errors, not a joint-likelihood SARIMAX fit. It matches ML's calendar and origin-weather inputs. All parameter choices use the same 21 earlier development days, with no evaluation retuning. Univariate smoothing/SARIMA and component-count VAR do not receive realised future weather; their narrower predictor sets are disclosed. Historical missing residuals retain their hourly positions in the ARIMA fit.
